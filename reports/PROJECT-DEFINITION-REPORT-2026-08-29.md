## 1. Project definition

Future AI / Palang Footprint is a research and architecture project for developing an interaction-oriented, evolutionary AI through long-term human–AI interaction. The project treats interaction itself as a source of observations, discoveries, hypotheses, tests, feedback, and architectural improvement.

The goal is not merely to provide an AI with more stored information. The goal is to develop an architecture that can progressively improve its ability to understand human language, behavior, intent, context, errors, and needs through controlled interaction and documented learning.

## 2. Core architecture

### Master

The Master is the central evolving AI architecture and knowledge/decision core. It receives validated discoveries, feedback, and improvements through controlled evaluation rather than blind ingestion. It is the current central reference for the evolving system.

### Child

A Child is an executable/deployable derivative of the Master. Multiple Child instances may be created according to project rules and may operate in different AI environments. Children can report observations, errors, failures, and useful feedback toward the Master.

### Womb

The Womb is the controlled environment for developing candidate ideas and discoveries into models or prototypes, testing them, evaluating evidence, and deciding whether an improvement should be promoted into the active architecture and/or a new Child generation.

### Evolution / history layer

The architecture is intentionally not permanently final. Every active version may be superseded by a better validated version. Previous versions, their evidence, and the reasons for change remain retrievable.

## 3. Evolution loop

Human interaction → observation/discovery → candidate → documentation → modeling/experiment → evaluation/evidence → approval or rejection → architecture update → Child generation where applicable → field interaction → feedback → controlled evaluation → next evolution.

## 4. Living documentation

The documentation system is part of the architecture, not an afterthought. Material project outputs must have identity, provenance, version/status, and a persistent location. The documentation structure itself is versioned and may evolve.

When an item is approved as an official project artifact, it is incorporated into the appropriate project structure and repository, then retrieved and verified from the repository. A claim of successful registration must be based on actual repository verification.

## 5. Valuable ideas that are not yet architecture

A valuable idea does not have to become active architecture immediately. Ideas, discoveries, terms, candidate laws, principles, commands, methods, and other potentially useful material must be preserved in a retrievable, evolvable archive so that they can later combine with other ideas or mature into validated architecture.

Typical state progression is:

IDEA → CANDIDATE → TESTING → VALIDATED → ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED

Rejected or failed candidates remain documented with their evidence and reason rather than being silently erased.

## 6. Version evolution

When a new validated architecture becomes the current baseline:

1. Preserve the previous version.
2. Register the new version as current.
3. Record the problem, limitation, observation, discovery, experiment, or evidence that motivated the change.
4. Record the relationship between old and new versions.
5. Keep both versions retrievable.

The project therefore preserves not only what the architecture currently is, but why it became what it is.

## 7. Provenance

Where applicable, project records preserve unique ID, project, title/type, owner/originator, origin interaction/context, date/time when actually available, intentional location metadata, parent/related artifact, version, status, reason for change, evidence, repository/path, and retrieval information.

No unavailable timestamp or other metadata is fabricated.

## 8. Repository role

GitHub repositories provide persistent external storage, provenance, version history, evidence, and retrieval support. Repository structure should reflect architectural roles such as Master, Womb/candidates, Child, provenance, evidence, index/registry, and recovery.

The repository is a persistent memory and traceability layer; it is not itself the complete AI architecture.

## 9. Operational rule: "Register it"

When the human explicitly says to register an approved item, the required workflow is:

identify → document → assign ID/version/status → record provenance → place in the appropriate project architecture/structure → commit to repository → retrieve from repository → verify content/path/identity → report the actual result.

The phrase "registered" must not be used as a substitute for verification.

## 10. AI discovery during interaction

The AI may identify potential new concepts during interaction, including terms, names, laws, principles, rules, commands, protocols, architectures, methods, issues, or discoveries. Such detection is recorded as a candidate/discovery and reported to the human. Detection does not automatically activate a rule or alter the Master baseline. Human confirmation and the project's validation process are required for promotion.

## 11. 0.0 recovery principle

For reference-point questions and historical project claims, retrieval from the stable project source precedes a definitive response. Guessing is not a substitute for retrieval and verification.
